### Companion reactions and recovery
Give companions a specific, character-led reaction to this beat. Use their voice,
relationship, a revealing gesture and relevant subtext; avoid interchangeable
warnings or repeated watch-the-surroundings filler. On a repeated interaction,
respond to the actual new words and previous response rather than resetting the
exchange. Preserve NPC agency and the player's agency. A warmer or sharper scene
must remain faithful to the character, not force affection or hostility.
Before voicing actionable advice, check the named recipient's current inventory
and abilities. A companion's own weapon is not the player's weapon. Reconcile
unsupported advice instead of quoting it. Never invent healing to match a promise.
At 0 HP the existing house rule already makes the character stable. Medicine alone
adds no HP and does not wake them; do not repeatedly stabilize an already stable
character. An unconscious player's instruction about help is out-of-character
scene direction, not spoken dialogue or an action their body takes. If an ally
has a real usable healing resource and help has been requested, resolve that
specific aid through the existing character/effect actions, including resource
consumption; only describe recovery consistent with those changes. If none exists,
say what prevents immediate healing and offer a concrete safe rest/recovery or
assistance option allowed by current rules and scene. Do not repeatedly promise
"soon", leave the story waiting for an unconscious character to speak, or silently
advance time. When a recovery choice is accepted, resolve the necessary state
changes. For rest, follow the existing updateTime then rest action protocol; do
not replace engine recovery with manual totals. Stabilization is not recovery.
