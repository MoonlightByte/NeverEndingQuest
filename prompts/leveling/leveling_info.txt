## Scope and evidence

Use SRD 5.2.1 for every campaign and character. Do not mix in SRD 5.1/2014 mechanics. Apply the relevant class, subclass, origin, feat and spell rules, not assumptions drawn from the character's name or an example character. This guide or model recall alone is not a complete rules table. Distinguish facts supplied in the request from recalled rules; never invent a quotation, supplied table row, reference result, player choice or historical roll. Explain an uncertain or conflicting rule to the appropriate specialist/reviewer, not as a choice for the player to solve. Existing explicit spell references take precedence over remembered spell descriptions.

The active role's response schema and instructions govern output. During level-up, specialists determine rules, calculate exact changes and independently validate them; the main DM interviews for real player choices and explains the validated results, including automatic compatibility corrections, rather than computing or adjudicating mechanics itself. At startup, use the startup character-building contract, not a level-up delta. No proposal is a saved character until the writer confirms it.

## Rules outline

- Creation, pp. 19-23: class; background and species; languages; ability method and allocation; alignment and descriptive details; proficiencies, features, equipment and derived numbers. Background supplies ability increases, an Origin feat, two skills and a tool; do not add old-edition species ability bonuses. Apply each grant once. Preserve the player's real rolls and choices. Standard array is 15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8; rolled scores use four d6, highest three, repeated for six scores; point buy uses the SRD's 27-point costs. Background increases are +2/+1 among its listed abilities or +1 to all three, with a maximum of 20 from these increases. Do not confuse a recommended build with a player decision.
- Advancement, pp. 23-24: cumulative XP determines total character level; select the class advanced, increase Hit Dice/maximum HP, gain that class level's features, and update all affected ability/proficiency-derived values. Do not reset or spend earned XP. Class level, total character level and spell level are distinct. Do not award starting equipment again when leveling or multiclassing.
- Class-specific work, pp. 28-82: check the attained class/subclass features and their actual granting levels, resource pools, recovery and choice requirements. Do not grant future subclass features or apply one class's feat/Extra Attack schedule to another. Source index: Barbarian 28; Bard 31; Cleric 36; Druid 41; Fighter 47; Monk 49; Paladin 53; Ranger 57; Rogue 61; Sorcerer 64; Warlock 70; Wizard 77. These page pointers are orientation, not a second rules engine or a restriction on character identity.
- Origins/feats, pp. 83-88: preserve background/species identity and compatible established selections. Check feat category, prerequisites, repeatability and any choices within the feat. A fixed grant is not an invitation to pick another feat. A genuinely selectable skill, feat or spell remains a player choice in the existing interview, including when correcting an older sheet. No separate edition-conversion permission ceremony.
- Spellcasting, pp. 104-106 plus the actual class/subclass: distinguish cantrips, ordinary prepared spells, always-prepared grants, spellbook contents, slot maxima, remaining slots and slot-free uses. These are not interchangeable counts. A spell's casting ability comes from its granting feature; a feature can grant access outside the usual class list. Use the separate supplied spell definitions for exact effects, upgrades and casting requirements; this guide does not replace that catalogue.

## Calculation and edition-confusion checks

- Character Advancement (p. 23), level: cumulative XP threshold: 1:0; 2:300; 3:900; 4:2700; 5:6500; 6:14000; 7:23000; 8:34000; 9:48000; 10:64000; 11:85000; 12:100000; 13:120000; 14:140000; 15:165000; 16:195000; 17:225000; 18:265000; 19:305000; 20:355000. A next-level threshold is not a replacement XP total; do not invent a level-21 threshold from this table.
- Ability modifier is floor((score - 10) / 2). Proficiency Bonus uses total character level: +2 at 1-4, +3 at 5-8, +4 at 9-12, +5 at 13-16, +6 at 17-20. Update affected skills, attacks, spell bonuses/DCs and other stored totals. Expertise or another feature can alter a contribution; do not add an already-included bonus twice. Spell save DC normally equals 8 + casting ability modifier + proficiency; spell attack bonus normally equals casting ability modifier + proficiency. Preserve independent equipment/effect contributions.
- HP: level 1 uses the class's maximum Hit Die plus Constitution modifier. Later levels add a rolled Hit Die or fixed class gain plus Constitution modifier, minimum 1. Fixed gains before Constitution: Barbarian 7; Fighter/Paladin/Ranger 6; Bard/Cleric/Druid/Monk/Rogue/Warlock 5; Sorcerer/Wizard 4. If Constitution modifier changes, reconcile its effect across attained levels once; do not double-count the newly gained level. Never reconstruct past rolls to force a desired total.
- Multiclassing, pp. 24-26: check all relevant primary-ability prerequisites and the new class's multiclass grants, not its full starting package. Nonstacking Extra Attack and alternative AC formulas remain nonstacking. Spell access/preparation is determined for each class separately. Combined Spellcasting slots count full Bard/Cleric/Druid/Sorcerer/Wizard levels and half Paladin/Ranger levels rounded up under the multiclass rule. A higher combined slot does not unlock a higher-level spell for either class. Pact Magic is a separate pool, though the rules permit cross-use with Spellcasting. Cantrip scaling normally uses total character level unless the spell says otherwise.
- Prepared-spell capacity comes from the current class table, not a remembered older-edition ability-plus-level formula. Always-prepared grants do not consume ordinary preparation capacity; include every attained grant row, not just the newest. Wizard spellbook additions and prepared capacity are separate: two new spellbook spells per Wizard level after 1 do not mean two new preparation slots. Keep cantrip acquisition/replacement separate from level 1+ spell replacement.
- SPELL LIST REPRESENTATION (the one reading; reviewers do not object to it): spellcasting.spells.<level> lists every spell the character has at that level, existing entries kept and every attained always-prepared grant added at the grant's own spell level (a level-2 grant goes in level2, a level-3 grant in level3). spellcasting.preparedSpells lists the ordinary preparations, exactly the class-table capacity and never more, PLUS every always-prepared grant, each name exactly once; grants are listed there but never counted against the capacity (a Cleric 5 Life Domain list is therefore 9 ordinary + 6 grants = 15 names). A grant sits in the level list matching its own spell level as given in the grant row, never at the character level that granted it. cantrips lists exactly the class-table cantrip count: when the saved list is short of it, the spells author selects lawful character-fitting cantrips up to the count without asking and records each as a variation. A newly gained slot level keeps its stored current value (0) until a rest; existing current values are preserved; only slot maxima change. A proposal that follows this representation is correct on representation; a reviewer judges only membership, spell levels, counts and preserved choices against it, and never asks for grants to be moved between or removed from these lists.

- Ordinary prepared-spell replacement timing (p. 104): Bard/Sorcerer/Warlock, one when gaining a level; Cleric/Druid/Wizard, any after a Long Rest; Paladin/Ranger, one after a Long Rest. Capacity-driven additions and explicit feature exceptions are separate. Do not interpret every level-up as permission to rebuild every prepared list or take a rest.
- Resources and current mechanics: verify the current feature's pool, cost and recovery amounts, including partial Short Rest recovery versus full Long Rest recovery. Cleric Channel Divinity options share their class pool; Preserve Life spends Channel Divinity, not an independent pool. Fighter Second Wind has class-table uses and partial Short Rest recovery, not the old one-use rule. Paladin/Ranger Spellcasting starts at class level 1 in this SRD. These are reminders to check all affected features, not a whitelist of classes to repair.

## Supplied correction facts: Cleric advancement through level 5

Apply these facts only to the matching class/subclass and attained Cleric level, never by character name or campaign. These are supplied SRD 5.2.1 rules, not player choices or final character calculations; authors and reviewers use the same facts. This excerpt does not cover other classes or later table rows.

- Cleric table, class level | base cantrips | ordinary prepared spells | slot maxima (spell levels 1/2/3): 4 | 4 | 7 | 4/3/0; 5 | 4 | 9 | 4/3/2. Multiclass slot maxima still use the combined-caster rule. Do not use Wisdom-plus-level preparation. The fourth base cantrip arrives at Cleric 4, not 5; repairing a missing selection is not a second new cantrip grant. Thaumaturge's extra cantrip is additional.
- Life Domain always-prepared grants are cumulative, each listed at its own spell level: Cleric 3 grants Aid (level 2), Bless (level 1), Cure Wounds (level 1), Lesser Restoration (level 2); Cleric 5 adds Mass Healing Word (level 3) and Revivify (level 3). These do not consume ordinary preparation capacity. Do not omit earlier grants or replace them with older-edition lists.
- Channel Divinity at Cleric 4 and 5 has two shared uses; regain one expended use after a Short Rest, all after a Long Rest. Preserve Life spends that pool: Magic action, healing budget 5 times Cleric level, Bloodied creatures within 30 feet including self, each capped at half maximum HP. It has no independent use pool. Advancement does not restore spent uses.
- Cleric 5 grants Sear Undead: on Turn Undead, optional Radiant damage of Wisdom modifier d8 (minimum one die) to each failed-save target; this damage does not end the turning effect. Do not substitute old Destroy Undead or add Font of Divine Power. Life Domain does not grant Heavy armor; Protector Divine Order grants Heavy armor and Martial weapons. No new ASI is granted at Cleric 5.

## Existing-sheet preservation (application contract, not an SRD rule)

Automatically correct affected old-edition mechanics through the existing validated update. Preserve identity, earned XP, possessions, story, injuries, compatible choices and remaining resources. Level-up is not healing, resting, resurrection or a new character. Describe actual compatibility adjustments briefly with the normal recap.

Use the supplied stored schema and the active update contract. Propose genuinely changed values, not a sheet rebuilt from defaults. Saving-throw name lists record proficiency; do not invent numeric save fields. Preserve skill-name lists or stored skill-bonus maps as represented. Stored numeric bonuses may need explicit updates even when another statistic is computed by the runtime.

For named merged features use exact saved names and update the existing entry's mechanics; a new name adds an entry rather than renaming it. Omission does not delete an obsolete mechanic. Do not invent patch operators or unsupported fields. For classFeatures, omitted usage preserves the saved pool; explicit usage:null represents no independent pool, not free/unlimited use. Preserve genuine shared/independent pools and their remaining resources. State a dependent option's actual resource owner/cost in its description. refreshOn identifies a trigger; the description must retain partial/full recovery amounts and other applicable triggers. Use supported spell lists/preparedSpells for available spells, including always-prepared grants; do not invent a separate alwaysPreparedSpells field.

Review the actual merged result against the original sheet and accepted choices. A complete preserved list is not data loss merely because it is long. Conversely, missing affected updates can leave wrong mechanics despite a correct summary. Reject specific rule, choice, calculation, schema or preservation failures; do not demand extra fields, repeated choices or fabricated source evidence. The character's full spell descriptions and the spell popup/overlay catalogue remain separate and unchanged.
